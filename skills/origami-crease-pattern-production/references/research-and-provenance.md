# Research and provenance

Use this reference only when the user asks to study an artist, blog, book, paper, or collection before creating a new model.

## Reading record

Record the source URL or file, article title, publication date when available, and what was actually inspected. Keep these scopes distinct:

- freshly read in the current task;
- inherited from an existing reading ledger;
- sampled because it is especially relevant;
- discovered but not read in full;
- inaccessible or unavailable.

Do not describe an inherited index as a fresh full reread. “All related blogs” needs a finite boundary: name the sites, capture the public item counts or inventory date, and say whether embedded media and second-hop links were included.

## What to extract

Extract decisions that transfer to a new design, such as:

- whether the designer starts from a face, silhouette, tree, or base;
- how ratios and active constraints determine paper allocation;
- how wings, legs, heads, or internal flaps connect through rivers;
- which failed prototypes revealed trapped layers or insufficient surface width;
- how local tests differ from validation of the whole CP.

Link each principle to the source that supports it. Keep short quotations within normal copyright limits and prefer paraphrase.

## Originality boundary

Do not trace, rename, or lightly alter a creator's CP and present it as a new model. Build a new feature plan and tree for the requested subject. If a known base or published construction is reused, identify it and describe the new contribution precisely.

Exclude downloaded article images and copied page archives from the delivery package unless the user owns them or their inclusion is clearly permitted. A reading ledger and source links are normally sufficient.
